SPINE §11 · addendum to the canonical design

Tiered Horizons and the Signal Architecture

Three horizons, three models, one shared discipline: an input is worth what its independent sourcing is worth, never what its volume suggests. Plus an audit of what already exists and can be taken off the shelf.

Revised 2026-09-19 after Phase 0 — two corrections reverse earlier claims in this document. §11.0.1 (new): n_eff is non-monotonic in weekly basket size, so the "15 markets/week" figure was assumed, not derived, and is actively harmful on a realistic cluster structure. §11.10: the PolyBench recommendation is retracted — it was made from search snippets, and reading the repository disproves it. Every other third-party project named in §11.10 is still described from public documentation only, with licenses, maintenance activity and API behaviour unverified; py_clob_client is the sole exception, its name confirmed in Phase 0(d).
§11.0

What the tiering changes

The revised scope bets where it can measure and only publishes where it cannot. The important consequence isn't that split — it's the arithmetic underneath it.

Design consequence

Market selection must actively maximise independence. Diversity across domain, geography and causal mechanism becomes a hard selection criterion, not a nicety. Fifteen markets about one election are one observation. Fifteen spanning a court ruling, a central bank decision, a sports outcome, a crypto threshold, a legislative vote and a weather event are close to fifteen.

11.0.1 Correction — n_eff is non-monotonic in basket size

Added after Phase 0(a) — reverses the figure above

r̄ is not a property of the venue that you discover and then live with. It is a property of the basket you choose, and it rises the moment you take more than one market per correlation cluster. Past k = the number of independent clusters available, r̄ grows faster than k does — so evidence per week falls while workload climbs.

Measured by phase0/screen_markets.py on a 20-market / 8-cluster fixture:

weekly basketr̄n_eff / weekweeks to n_eff = 247

80.0505.9342 ← optimum

150.2073.8564
200.3152.8786

Twenty markets a week is 2.5× the work of eight for half the evidence, and doubles the time to a verdict. Three consequences:

  1. The weekly basket size is not a free parameter. It should equal the number of independent clusters the screen finds, and no more. Taking a round number like 15 because it sounds productive is a way to spend months buying correlated duplicates.
  2. Volume within a domain is worthless. The lever that shortens Register A is cluster count — more regions, more mechanisms, more question types. Trading more markets about the same handful of theses accumulates work, not evidence.
  3. r̄ ≤ 0.1 is achievable by construction, not by luck: take one market per cluster and observed r̄ stays at the cross-cluster floor. So the binding question is not "how correlated is Polymarket" but "how many distinct clusters does it offer per week."

11.0.2 The honest cost of tiering

Three tiers means three models, not one model at three horizons. T1 — will X happen in ten days — does not use Stage 2 pressure vectors; five-year debt trajectories say nothing about a court ruling next Thursday. T1 runs on event-proximate signal: scheduled calendars, news flow, short-event base rates, market microstructure.

Must not be forgotten

Calibration earned in T1 does not transfer to T3. Passing Register A on 250 short-horizon markets demonstrates the T1 model is calibrated. It says nothing about whether the structural method works, because the structural method was not used.

Publishing a T3 forecast alongside a T1 track record invites exactly the inference SPINE exists to prevent — spending Layer 3 credibility on Layer 1 claims. Every published T3 forecast carries the disclosure that its horizon class has no resolved track record.

§11.1

Signal count is not signal evidence

The de-duplication problem and the n_eff problem are the same problem at opposite ends of the pipeline.

Stage 4 exists because twenty forecasts downstream of one thesis are not twenty pieces of evidence. The identical distortion applies to inputs: fifty articles about one event are not fifty signals, and five outlets running the same wire copy are not five sources. So the signal layer reuses the machinery already specified rather than inventing a parallel one:

n_eff_sources(cluster) = n / (1 + (n − 1) · r̄_source)      -- Kish, again

One story carried by AP and forty syndicating outlets yields n_eff_sources ≈ 1. The same story independently reported by Reuters, a local outlet with its own correspondent, and a primary filing yields ≈ 3. Everything downstream weights on n_eff_sources, never on article count.

§11.3

The pipeline

Ten stages. Each emits provenance; nothing is discarded silently, because "we filtered it" must be auditable afterwards.

S0  Ingest          raw item + source + first_seen_at (immutable)
S1  Canonicalise    boilerplate strip, text normalise, content_hash
S2  Exact dedup     content_hash collision → drop, record pointer
S3  Near-dup        MinHash/LSH → near-duplicate groups
S4  Event cluster   embeddings + density clustering → event_cluster_id
S5  Syndication     collapse wire copies and same-owner outlets to originator
S6  Independence    compute n_eff_sources(cluster) from co-publication history
S7  Claim extract   structured claim + primary-source binding attempt
S8  Verify          corroboration (independence-weighted), entailment, contradiction
S9  Emit            signal record → point-in-time feature store
The stage that cannot be got wrong

first_seen_at is the moment we first observed the item. Stamped once, never revised, never backfilled. Publication timestamps from sources are recorded separately and treated as claims, not facts — outlets silently re-date articles. Every downstream point-in-time query keys on first_seen_at.

§11.4

Deduplication and source independence

Three layers, three techniques

LayerWhat it catchesTechnique
Byte-identicalRe-crawls, feed repeatsSHA-256 content hash
Near-duplicateWire copy with edited headline, truncation, boilerplate driftMinHash + LSH over shingles, Jaccard ≈ 0.8
Semantic / same-eventIndependent reporting of one underlying eventSentence embeddings + density clustering

These must be separate passes. MinHash will not catch two genuinely different articles about one event; embedding clustering is too expensive and too fuzzy to be the first line against literal re-crawls.

Event clustering — two required adaptations

Documented practice for continuous news streams is embeddings → UMAP → HDBSCAN. Two changes are needed here:

  1. Streaming, not batch. Clusters accept new members continuously without re-clustering history — re-clustering would retroactively change past feature values and destroy point-in-time correctness. Periodic offline re-fit writes a new cluster version rather than mutating the old one.
  2. Time-boxed. Two events months apart can be textually near-identical ("Fed holds rates"). Membership requires temporal proximity as well as semantic similarity.

Estimating source correlation

r̄_source is learned, not assumed. Build a source × event-cluster incidence matrix over history; for each pair compute co-publication association (φ or Jaccard over clusters both covered, conditioned on cluster size so sources that cover everything aren't rewarded). Sources that nearly always co-occur within minutes are syndication partners — correlation near 1. Feed the pairwise matrix into Kish per cluster.

A useful by-product: a publication-lag profile per source. A source that consistently publishes an event first is a genuinely independent reporter; one that consistently publishes forty minutes later is a follower and is discounted accordingly.

Syndication collapse

Before independence weighting, explicitly collapse known wire originators and their carriers (detect via byline, dateline, meta attribution, and near-dup grouping to the earliest member); same-owner outlet families, since a media group running one story across twelve mastheads is one source; and aggregators and republishers, which are never counted as sources. Ownership mapping is external data and has to be maintained.

§11.5

Noise removal

Volume is not information

News volume about an entity spikes for reasons unrelated to any change in probability — anniversary coverage, media cascades, a slow news day, an unrelated story pulling the entity into frame. Raw mention counts are among the worst features available and are exactly what naive pipelines key on.

Normalise against a rolling baseline for that entity and topic, and use deviation from expected volume. Flag cascade patterns — rapid growth with n_eff_sources staying flat — as amplification rather than new information. That signature is precisely one story being repeated, and it should reduce confidence in a volume signal, not raise it.

§11.5.2 · The reflexivity trap

Filter news about the market itself. "Polymarket odds shift on X," "traders now price Y at 68%" — that is the market talking to itself. Ingesting it as evidence and then scoring against that same market creates a feedback loop that looks like signal in backtest and is nothing.

Maintain an explicit exclusion class for any item whose primary subject is a prediction-market price, betting odds, or forecast aggregate. Store it — useful for measuring attention — but never route it into the likelihood update. Prediction-market coverage is now common enough that this is a real contaminant, not a theoretical one.

FilterRationale
Opinion / analysis vs reportageCommentary restates known facts; not new evidence
Speculation markers"could", "may", "reportedly" — downweighted, not dropped
Recycled / anniversary contentHigh similarity to items >30 days old with no new claim

§11.6

Verification

Four checks, in increasing cost.

  1. Primary-source binding. Resolve every claim to an official artifact where possible — a filing, a docket entry, an agency release. A bound claim needs no corroboration. An unbound claim is a report about a fact, not the fact.
  2. Independence-weighted corroboration. Confidence scales with n_eff_sources, never article count. A claim in forty syndicated copies of one wire story is a single-source claim.
  3. Entailment check. Does the extracted claim actually follow from the source text? Cheap NLI models catch extraction errors and headline/body mismatch, a large category.
  4. Contradiction check. Does this contradict something asserted earlier with comparable support? Contradictions queue for adjudication; both claims are held at reduced weight until resolved.
The rule that governs all four

For a market being traded, the only verification that ultimately matters is what the market's own resolution source says. A claim can be true, well-corroborated, and irrelevant because the oracle reads a different document. Signals inform the forecast; the market's resolution text defines the question.

§11.7

Point-in-time discipline

Where most backtests of news-driven systems quietly fail — the same failure mode as revised macro data in §5.3.

  1. first_seen_at is immutable, set once at ingestion.
  2. Feature values are never recomputed in place. A model change emits a new feature version; old values stay queryable as they were.
  3. Every retrieval is a point-in-time join — "what was known as of T," never "what the current table says about T."
  4. Cluster assignments are versioned. Re-clustering creates a new version; it never rewrites history.
  5. Source reliability priors are themselves point-in-time. A source's score in March 2026 must be the score computed from data available in March 2026, not today's score applied retroactively.

Rule 5 is the subtle one and it is routinely violated. If source reliability is learned from outcomes, applying today's learned reliability to a 2024 backtest leaks outcome information backwards.

Don't hand-roll this

Point-in-time correct feature retrieval is the defining primitive of a feature store. It is a solved problem in MLOps — see §11.10.

§11.8

From signals to a forecast

Signals do not set probabilities. They update a prior, in log-odds, with bounded influence.

logit(p_t) = logit(p_0) + λ_sig · Σ_c  w_c · LLR_c

w_c   = log(1 + n_eff_sources(c)) / log(1 + N_ref)     -- diminishing returns
LLR_c ∈ [−L_max, +L_max]                                -- per-cluster ceiling
  • Corroboration has diminishing returns. The second independent source moves the estimate a lot; the tenth barely. The logarithm encodes that — and it is why n_eff_sources must be right.
  • No single story can dominate. L_max ≈ 1.5 caps one cluster at roughly a 4.5:1 likelihood ratio.
  • Summation is over clusters, not articles. Volume cannot substitute for independence.
  • The firewall still binds. After all updates the §4 CHECK clamps the result into the layer's symmetric band. No quantity of news promotes a T1 claim past 0.60 — the existing control doing its job on a new input path, which is why the signal layer needs no separate cap.

λ_sig is calibrated exactly as λ is in §5.3 — three splits, frozen, versioned on every forecast. It is a second free parameter and carries the same abuse risk.

§11.12

Risks specific to the signal layer

  1. r̄ between markets is the whole timeline. If the T1 universe is dominated by correlated political questions, n_eff accumulates slowly and Phase 3 stretches back toward years. Measure before committing.
  2. Reflexivity contamination will look like signal in backtest. If prediction-market commentary leaks into the likelihood update, the system learns to read its own reflection and the error is invisible until live trading.
  3. Retroactive source scores leak outcomes backwards. Subtle, easy to implement by accident, fatal to any backtest result.
  4. Two calibrated free parameters double the surface for tuning-until-it-looks-right. Both need frozen splits and committed configs.
  5. Ingestion cost and rate limits. Continuous multi-source news ingestion at useful latency is an ongoing operational cost, not a one-off build.
  6. Legal and terms-of-service constraints on scraping. Unexamined here. Must be checked before any crawler runs.

Schema additions for signal_items, event_clusters, cluster_members, source_correlation and signals are in §11.9 of the Markdown companion. Note there is no UPDATE path for signals — re-evaluation emits a new row with a new feature_version, exactly as forecast_neff_history does for n_eff.
